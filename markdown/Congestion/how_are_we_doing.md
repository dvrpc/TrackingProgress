The first chart shows the highway Planning Time Index (PTI) averaged over the whole year for a 24-hour period. The region’s PTI reached a new peak of 1.49 in 2024, a 9.6% increase since 2021. The lowest regional highway PTI occurred in 2020 at 1.20 when travel was greatly reduced due to the shutdowns caused by the Covid-19 pandemic. Local roads’ PTI has decreased since their peak in 2014 and 2015 (1.39), except for slight increases in 2019 and 2023. Chester and Gloucester Counties most frequently rank as most reliable in the index, and Philadelphia as the least. Weekday evening rush hour is when travel is the least reliable, particularly on highways. Trips have become more spread throughout the day since the pandemic. As a result, midday PTI, particularly on highways, has generally been increasing, and now looks like AM peak PTI.

The second chart shows in 2024, 78.9% of the region’s interstate roadways had reliable travel conditions, which is down from 81.0% in 2023. This performance was better than prepandemic ranges of 73.3% to 75.0%, but below the 2020 reliability peak of 90.5%. Non-Interstate percent reliability increased in the region with the chart showing 90.7% in 2023 and 92.2% in 2024. Philadelphia has the lowest percent of reliable interstates (51.2%) and non-interstates (84.4%).

The third chart shows that the region’s Truck Travel Time Reliability (TTTR) was 1.72 in 2024—which is better than the worst performing year (1.91 in 2018), but above the prior year (2023) with a value of 1.67 and above the best performing year, 2020 with a value of 1.55. Philadelphia had the highest TTTR in the region from 2017 and 2024.

The fourth chart shows annual hours of peak hour excessive delay (PHED). The Philadelphia, PA-NJ-DE-MD Urban Area had 12.9 annual hours of PHED per capita in 2024, down from 13.9 in 2023, or a 7.2% decrease. The Trenton, NJ Urban Area had 4.9 annual hours of PHED per capita in 2024, which represented a new peak.

The fifth chart shows the highest peak hour PTI for the region’s bus routes. The routes have generally become more unreliable since 2021, with PTIs increasing on average by 6.6% per year from 2021 to 2024. From 2023 to 2024, however, NJ Transit and SEPTA’s City Transit Division routes improved in reliability, with PTIs decreasing by 2.6% and 4.8% respectively.
