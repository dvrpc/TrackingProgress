The Income Concentration Index measures how evenly households of different income levels are distributed across census tracts in the region. It is based on a statistical approach known as a dissimilarity index. The index estimates the share of residents who would need to relocate to achieve an even distribution of households by income across communities. Lower values indicate a more even distribution, while higher values indicate that households are more concentrated by income in certain areas. For this measure, low-income households are defined as those earning below 200% of the federal poverty level. The index uses decennial census tract data for 1990 and 2000. The U.S. Census Bureau’s American Communities Survey (ACS) five-year period estimates are used for subsequent tract analysis; the final year of the period is displayed as the data year (for example, 2022 represents the 2018–2022 period).
