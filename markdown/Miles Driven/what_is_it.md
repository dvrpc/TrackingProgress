Daily vehicle miles traveled (VMT) is a distance and volume-based measure, quantifying the total distance, in miles, driven by all motorized vehicle types—car, bus, motorcycle, and truck— on an average day. Daily VMT per capita is the same measure divided by the same area's population for the same year. Daily VMT per vehicle divides daily VMT by the number of household vehicles available by residents of that geography in the same year. Normalizing daily VMT by a county or region's population, or household vehicles, is helpful for context, but does not have complete parity with what is measured in daily VMT estimates. People and vehicles come into the region from other places, just as people and vehicles leave the region to visit other places. Daily VMT per capita compares all miles traveled on the region's roads to the region's population (for all ages) from the U.S. Census Bureau's latest population estimates. Vehicle counts for daily VMT are classified by vehicle types, but not by vehicle ownership. In 2024, statewide estimates for daily VMT by motorcycles, passenger cars, and two-axle single-unit trucks with four wheels made up 88% of Pennsylvania's daily VMT, and 94% of New Jersey's. These vehicle types are highly likely to be personal vehicles, owned by households, but a small percent could be fleet vehicles of companies or governments. The remaining daily VMT is made up of vehicle types like school and commercial buses and trucks with more than two axles, which are generally commercial vehicles.
