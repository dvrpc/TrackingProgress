Risks from natural hazards and severe weather events due to global warming pose threats to both the environment and the economy. It is important to keep the global climate system stable to support human civilization without radical changes to economic, agricultural, transportation, and other systems. The *Connections 2050* Plan sets a goal to reduce regional carbon dioxide (CO<sub>2</sub>) emissions to net zero by 2050.

Since the start of the industrial age, the increasing combustion of fossil fuels has increased the generation of CO<sub>2</sub>, leading to rising global temperatures and potentially destabilizing the earth’s climate. A destabilized climate threatens the viability of human life on the planet. Reducing the accumulation of heat-trapping gases in the atmosphere is critical for slowing or even reversing global temperature increases. Many of the actions taken to accomplish this goal will also make our transportation network more efficient, improve air quality, bolster public health, and make the region's communities more livable. An efficient regional economy that emits fewer heat-trapping gases will also have multiple advantages in an increasingly competitive economic world.

Increased CO<sub>2</sub> in the atmosphere results in higher average global temperature, increases in the amount of water in the world’s oceans, and more extreme weather events. These changing weather patterns present a major fairness issue, not only globally, but within our region. Many of our region’s poorest communities face greater risks from extreme heat due to a lack of tree canopy, vegetation, and greenspace in their communities, as well as higher levels of existing air pollution. Wealthier individuals are also able to afford housing that is better insulated and sealed, and are more likely to have and be able to afford to use air conditioning.
