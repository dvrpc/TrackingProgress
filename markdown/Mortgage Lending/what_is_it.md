Mortgage lending information comes from the Federal Financial Institutions Examination Council's (FFIEC) Home Mortgage Disclosure Act (HMDA) data. *Tracking Progress* includes data on *loan originations*—the creation of a loan after bank approval to purchase a home, undertake home improvements, or refinance an existing mortgage—and *application denials*—when a financial institution decides not to make a loan to an applicant for these same purposes. 

The first chart shows gaps in loan origination rate trends by sex, ethnicity, and race. Each group’s loan origination rates are shown in the second chart. Total *decisioned* applications for each year are shown in the final chart, which shows the volume of attempted loan activity occurring within each group. Loan origination rates are calculated from the number of loan applications that are approved (*originated*) divided by the total applications submitted— called *decisioned applications* in the third chart.

By default, each chart presents gaps between or trendlines for female, male, Latinx, and non-white. To view the gaps or trendlines for component non-white groups, select the radio button for Asian or Black. The loan’s purpose can be selected via a right dropdown list in all charts.

*Latinx* represents loan recipients identifying as Hispanic or Latino. Racial groups are presented as non-white and white groups, respectively, and contain both Latinx and non-Latinx populations. Races included as non-white are Black, Asian, and populations that were too small to show individual trends, like American Indian and Alaska natives or Native Hawaiian and other Pacific Islanders.

HMDA data limits sex to a binary scale. Choices to survey respondents are limited to male, female, or to leave the information blank. The binary choice in the survey does not capture data specific to intersex and trans individuals; those who identify with a different gender (transgender); or persons who are gender non-conforming, genderqueer, gender fluid, or non-binary. Since the data refers to sex, the same term is used, knowing it has limits. The term sex is also in line with the protections against discrimination based on sex found in the Federal-Aid Highway Act of 1973—a law DVRPC must comply with as a public agency receiving federal funds.

HMDA data includes six other actions that aren’t included in Tracking Progress for when an applicant withdraws their application or does not accept the approved loan, the file is closed due to incomplete information, a loan is purchased by another institution, or a pre-approval request is denied or approved but not accepted. This data does not include those who can pay cash for home purchases or home improvements, and thus do not need to make a mortgage loan application. 

Applicants are not required to fill out demographic data. Since 2018, between 16.2% and 28% percent of applications lack some combination or all of the information requested for sex, race, and ethnicity. Prior to 2018, these questions did not include a ‘not applicable’ option and more people filled out this information. Between just 6.5% and 18.6% of applications left these fields blank.
